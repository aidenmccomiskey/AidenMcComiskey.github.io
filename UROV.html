Underwater ROV

From a no-budget middle-school club to the MATE World Championships.

Role
Team lead and designer
Team
6 people
Dates
2017 – 2023
Result
1st, New England (Navigators); MATE Worlds

Background

I joined an underwater robotics club in middle school with no budget: we built our first ROV from old bilge pumps and kitchen tongs. In high school I got the club recognised as an academic course, which later became the school’s most popular minor.

Leading a team of 6, I helped design and build an ROV that won 1st place in the Navigators Division of the New England regional and competed at the MATE World Championships.

My role

As team lead I split the team into groups, set milestones for regional and international competitions, and presented our work to judges and industry professionals. I also led much of the engineering:

  • Frame

    Designed and 3D-printed a lightweight, modular frame from PLA and our own PET filament made from recycled bottles. It switches between a high-payload setup and a lightweight, high-speed configuration.

  • Thrusters

    Designed custom thrusters that cut power use, improved manoeuvrability, and brought the cost from $200 to $20 each.

  • Robotic arm

    Used generative design to cut the arm’s weight while keeping its strength.

  • Electronics

    Supervised integration of power, control boards, and waterproofing. An onboard Raspberry Pi with a custom tether handles two-way communication, driven by an Xbox controller; a Pixhawk running ArduSub provides auto-levelling.
