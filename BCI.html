Transformer model for a brain-computer interface

Decoding electrocorticogram (ECoG) signals into text, fast enough to personalise the model while a patient uses it.

Advisor
Prof. Joseph G. Makin, Purdue
Dates
2021 – 2023
Result
95.5% test accuracy
Award
Silver, Computational Biology, Regeneron WESEF

Motivation

When I was 12, my uncle was diagnosed with Huntington’s disease. I watched him gradually lose the ability to move and eventually to communicate. He tried to get access to a BCI to restore his communication but wasn’t able to. That is what drew me to the field.

Research

Working with data provided by Professor Joseph Makin, I replaced the recurrent neural network previously used to decode ECoG signals with a transformer model, originally designed for natural language processing.

Results

  • Accuracy

    95.5% test accuracy on sentence decoding, comparable to state-of-the-art approaches while needing significantly less training data.

  • Training time

    15 minutes and 25 epochs instead of 8 hours, 32× faster. That makes it practical to train the decoder on a patient while they are using it.

  • Neural signals as language

    The premise was that neural signals share structure with human language. Treating ECoG as sequential data for a transformer supported that in practice.

Impact

Faster, more efficient BCIs could restore communication for people with ALS, Huntington’s disease, or severe paralysis. The work won the silver medal in Computational Biology at the Regeneron Westchester Science and Engineering Fair.
